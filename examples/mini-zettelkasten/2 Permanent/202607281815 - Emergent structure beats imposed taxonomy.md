# Emergent structure beats imposed taxonomy

A taxonomy designed upfront can only file the ideas you already expected
to have. A network that grows bottom-up — each new note linked to whatever
it genuinely relates to — can surface clusters nobody planned. The
slip-box's value comes precisely from those unplanned clusters: structure
that emerges from use fits the material; structure imposed in advance
fits only the designer's past assumptions.

This is why Luhmann never sorted cards by topic. He placed each new card
*behind* a card it related to, letting neighborhoods form on their own.

## Why it matters

It explains why every carefully foldered note system I built eventually
died: the filing scheme was always about who I was when I designed it,
not about what the notes were actually saying.
